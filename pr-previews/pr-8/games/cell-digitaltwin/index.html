Cell Digital Twin

A quantitative, literature-grounded model of a living cell: the concentrations of ions and metabolites across the membrane, and the transport proteins that move them. Choose a cell type, run the simulation, and work through the guided lessons. Erythrocyte homeostasis after Lew & Bookchin (1986) with glycolysis from Mulquiney & Kuchel (1999); the cardiomyocyte action potential after Luo & Rudy (1991).       About this model

      A learning tool, not a clinical model. State is integrated as conserved amounts so total ion content and charge are preserved (verified in the test suite →). The membrane potential is solved at quasi-steady state each step; the red cell sits near its Donnan/Goldman equilibrium, set by the dominant Cl⁻ permeability of Band 3.

      Erythrocyte transporters modelled: Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, PMCA Ca²⁺ pump, Band 3 (Cl⁻/HCO₃⁻ antiport), GLUT1, the Gardos K⁺ channel, and passive Na⁺/K⁺ leaks, coupled to anaerobic glycolysis and the 2,3-BPG shunt.

      The cardiomyocyte is the Luo–Rudy phase I (1991) guinea-pig ventricular model: the fast Na⁺ current (I_Na), slow inward Ca²⁺ current (I_si), delayed and inward-rectifier K⁺ currents (I_K, I_K1), the plateau K⁺ current (I_Kp), and a background leak, with six Hodgkin–Huxley gates advanced by Rush–Larsen and a dynamic [Ca²⁺]i. Press ⚡ Stimulate (or pace) to fire an action potential. Extending toward ten Tusscher 2006 (explicit SR Ca²⁺ handling) is the planned next step.
