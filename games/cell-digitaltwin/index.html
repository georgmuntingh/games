Cell Digital Twin

A quantitative, literature-grounded model of a living cell: the concentrations of ions and metabolites across the membrane, and the transport proteins that move them. Choose a cell type, run the simulation, and work through the guided lessons. Erythrocyte homeostasis after Lew & Bookchin (1986) with glycolysis from Mulquiney & Kuchel (1999); the cardiomyocyte action potential after Luo & Rudy (1991); and a myelinated neuron — a multi-compartment Hodgkin–Huxley cable (Pospischil 2008; McIntyre–Richardson–Grill 2002) with saltatory conduction.       About this model

      A learning tool, not a clinical model. State is integrated as conserved amounts so total ion content and charge are preserved (verified in the test suite →). The membrane potential is solved at quasi-steady state each step; the red cell sits near its Donnan/Goldman equilibrium, set by the dominant Cl⁻ permeability of Band 3.

      Erythrocyte transporters modelled: Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, PMCA Ca²⁺ pump, Band 3 (Cl⁻/HCO₃⁻ antiport), GLUT1, the Gardos K⁺ channel, and passive Na⁺/K⁺ leaks, coupled to anaerobic glycolysis and the 2,3-BPG shunt.

      The cardiomyocyte is the Luo–Rudy phase I (1991) guinea-pig ventricular model: the fast Na⁺ current (I_Na), slow inward Ca²⁺ current (I_si), delayed and inward-rectifier K⁺ currents (I_K, I_K1), the plateau K⁺ current (I_Kp), and a background leak, with six Hodgkin–Huxley gates advanced by Rush–Larsen and a dynamic [Ca²⁺]i. Press ⚡ Stimulate (or pace) to fire an action potential. Extending toward ten Tusscher 2006 (explicit SR Ca²⁺ handling) is the planned next step.

      The neuron is a multi-compartment Hodgkin–Huxley cable: dendrites, soma, axon hillock and a myelinated axon of alternating nodes of Ranvier and internodes. Each compartment carries fast Na⁺ (m³h), delayed-rectifier K⁺ (n⁴), leak, L-type Ca²⁺, Ca²⁺-activated K⁺ (SK), transient A-type K⁺ and the H-current, plus an electrogenic Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, with its own dynamic [Na⁺]/[K⁺]/[Ca²⁺] and neighbours coupled by axial resistance (cable theory). Voltage and concentrations are advanced by RK4, the gates by Rush–Larsen. Kinetics follow Pospischil et al. (2008) at 37 °C (Q10-scaled); node/internode properties follow McIntyre–Richardson–Grill (2002). The membrane is colour-mapped by local Vm so the action potential is visible jumping node to node; the lower trace shows Vm along the axon. Press ⚡ Stimulate, inject a tonic current, add EPSP/IPSP input, or click a compartment to move the injection site. The node compartments are coarsened above their ~1 µm anatomy so the explicit cable stays numerically stable.

      The mitochondrion is the Cortassa–Aon–Marbán–Winslow–O'Rourke (2003) integrated model: the full TCA cycle (citrate synthase through malate dehydrogenase, with Ca²⁺-sensitive isocitrate and α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenases) feeds NADH/FADH₂ to the electron-transport chain, which pumps protons to charge the inner-membrane potential ΔΨm; the F₁F₀-ATP synthase, adenine nucleotide translocase and proton leak discharge it, and the Ca²⁺ uniporter and Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchanger set matrix Ca²⁺. Every TCA, oxidative-phosphorylation and Ca²⁺ equation and parameter is taken from the model's CellML encoding; two documented curation fixes are applied (the Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchanger direction and a consistent 3 H⁺:ATP coupling of the synthase). A reactive-oxygen-species module (after Cortassa 2004 / Zhou 2009) and a Ca²⁺/ROS-gated permeability transition pore (after Pokhilko 2006 / Bernardi 2015) use the published functional forms with parameters calibrated for stable, visible behaviour. Add ADP to drive state-3 respiration, block a complex (rotenone, cyanide, oligomycin), uncouple with FCCP, or overload with Ca²⁺ to open the pore. State variables are bounded to their conserved pools each step.
