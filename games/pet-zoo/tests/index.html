Pet Zoo tests

The clock's angle maths and 5-minute snapping, the hour hand's coupling to the minutes, the grading verdicts behind the gentle correction, the two-phase spaced-repetition schedule and its scheduler, tier unlocking, the micro-session caps and the reload-proof nap, the subject registry that lets one zoo teach more than one thing, the upgrade that carries an older save forward instead of discarding it, and the storage layer's refusal to throw. For maths: that the thirty-one rungs partition the deck so each one can actually be finished, that every difference has an addition fact to lean on, that the generated column sums are reproducible and really do produce the case they were asked for, that a skill cannot graduate until it has got every case inside it right, the verdict behind each correction — checked by running the mistaken procedure and feeding its output back to the grader — the ten-frame's filling and emptying, the column walkthrough's carries, and the answer strip's refusal to hint at how big the answer is. For the times tables: that a pair is one pet however it is written, that a missing factor is asked from both ends, that the six strategy rungs hold the pairs they were designed to, and that the array drawn over a wrong product really is the question as it was written. For multiplying with the numbers stacked: that the rows of the working are the two partial products and their total, that every step of the method agrees with the answer it comes to, that a forgotten shift is named rather than merely marked wrong, that every row has to be right, and that the walkthrough walks the one row that went wrong. For the eggs: that every pet's egg is stable and unlike its neighbour's in size, shape, pattern and colour at once, that a full zoo meets every one of them, and that not one speckle hangs off the side of a shell. For the save: that renaming the subject carries a grown-up's choices across and does not pay a child twice for tiers they finished months ago, and that not one pet in an existing zoo changes species, name or colour under any of it. For handwriting: the stroke maths, the rasteriser's agreement with MNIST's own framing, the JavaScript forward pass against the numpy one that trained it, the reading of a backwards digit, and what the save keeps of a child's handwriting — and refuses to carry to anybody else's device. Rendering, dragging, the keypad and the writing pads are not covered here.
